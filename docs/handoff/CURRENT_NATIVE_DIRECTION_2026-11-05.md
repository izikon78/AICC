# Current research direction: trained-native AIN

This supplements the dated conversation reconstruction without claiming to import
the complete original chat. The user's current goal is to determine whether AIN
learned before inference reduces AI usage, and whether AICC adds later value.

Read in order:

1. [Current three hypotheses](../ain/NATIVE_HYPOTHESES.md).
2. [Feasibility decision and primary sources](../research/NATIVE_AIN_FEASIBILITY_2026-11-05.md).
3. [Draft trained-native protocol](../ain/TRAINED_NATIVE_PILOT_PROTOCOL.md).
4. [Article Draft 9](../article/AICC_Article_Draft_9.md).

Fixed language grammar/core operations are learned during training. A per-task
language primer is not part of that condition. New capability/task/version/private
interface information can still need context. Equal data, compute, constraints,
libraries and tuning opportunities matter; a conventional IR with good tokens is
a required strong control.

The original processing experiments, 867-parameter selector, numeric interpreter
timings and small prompted-edit pilot did not train an AIN model. They are retained
engineering evidence. Current-tokenizer codebook counts cannot validate or reject
the trained-native claim. Neither can an earlier contributor's opinion label.

The next run needs a real trainable checkpoint/tokenizer interface, frozen data
and judges, actual resource accounting and an agreed bounded training setup.
No paid model/API/training run was begun by this reassessment. Do not turn this
research recommendation into authorization for unbounded spending.

Keep historical source/results unchanged. The Claude branch is a separate
prototype with useful mutation tests and reproduced judge gaps; it has not been
silently merged or corrected. Repair/port those cases before making the judge a
research gate. Preserve Izik Bachar's concept/direction credit and the exact
requested beep opening. External papers establish prior mechanisms, not AIN's
specific advantage or priority over the field.
